Post 1 — Publication announcement

Caption
A citable party-position panel is now available for research.
It covers 708 parties in 65 countries from 1944 to 2025.

The panel contains 4,924 election-year rows, with one row per party segment at an election. For each estimate, it records economic left-right and cultural cosmopolitan-traditionalist positions on bounded 0 to 1 scales, posterior uncertainty intervals, and metadata showing which evidence informed the estimate. Uncertainty ships with every position rather than being left out of the dataset.

The estimates come from a Bayesian dynamic item-response model that combines manifesto and media text codings with expert survey placements. The release is research infrastructure: a structured, documented panel that researchers can inspect and use in their own analyses.

Post 2 — Interactive explorer

Caption
A position estimate is more informative when its interval is visible.
The explorer makes it possible to inspect both together.

In the party2d interactive explorer, readers can add parties, switch between the economic and cultural dimensions, and see intervals widen where evidence is thin. The intervals are part of the information in the panel, not decoration around a single point: they show the posterior uncertainty associated with each estimated position.

The panel covers 708 parties in 65 countries from 1944 to 2025. Each election-year row records economic left-right and cultural cosmopolitan-traditionalist positions on bounded 0 to 1 scales, together with the evidence metadata used to inform the estimate. Estimates come from a Bayesian dynamic item-response model combining manifesto and media text codings with expert survey placements.

Post 3 — Descriptive statistics

Caption
What does the evidence behind a party-position panel look like?
The mix and interval widths help put the estimates in context.

Across 4,924 election-year rows, 59.7 percent combine text and expert evidence, 33.8 percent rely on text alone, 4.8 percent on expert evidence alone, and 1.7 percent are temporally propagated. The median 95 percent interval width is 0.224 on the economic dimension and 0.193 on the cultural dimension, on bounded 0 to 1 scales.

These figures describe the evidence mix and the spread of posterior estimates; they do not make every row equally well supported. The panel covers 708 parties across 65 countries from 1944 to 2025, but it is Europe-heavy. It combines manifesto and media text codings with expert survey placements in a Bayesian dynamic item-response model, and includes evidence metadata alongside each party-segment election row.
