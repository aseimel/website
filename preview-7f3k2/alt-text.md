# Alt text for the party2d launch images

Paste the matching line into the LinkedIn image alt field. Each line describes what the image shows, not why it matters.

## Post 1, publication carousel (party2d-post1-publication.pdf, 6 pages)

1. Title slide reading 708 parties, 65 countries, 1944 to 2025, with the line that the panel carries an uncertainty interval for every estimate and notes on the evidence behind it.
2. Slide headed "Expert surveys and text codings do not share a scale", explaining that expert placements are precise but sparse in time while text codings reach further back with their own measurement error.
3. Slide headed "One measurement model pools both", listing hurdle binomial items for text counts, beta-binomial items for expert means, and random walks within party segments.
4. Table showing the fields of one row: Social Democratic Party, Germany, 2021, economic position 0.24 with interval 0.15 to 0.33, cultural position 0.37 with interval 0.28 to 0.46, informed by one text source and three expert sources.
5. Slide with the figure 33.8 percent, headed "One in three estimates rests on text evidence alone", noting that intervals widen where expert evidence is missing and that a further 1.7 percent of rows are propagated in time.
6. Slide listing the article DOI, the CC0 dataset DOI, the Codeberg v1 release and the interactive explorer.

## Post 2, explorer carousel (party2d-post2-explorer.pdf, 3 pages)

1. Title slide reading "Two parties. Two dimensions.", stating that every line carries its 95 percent interval and that the explorer covers 708 parties.
2. Screenshot of the Party2D explorer with the Austrian Freedom Party and the German Social Democrats selected, showing economic positions as solid lines and cultural positions as dashed lines on one shared 0 to 1 scale, each line surrounded by a shaded 95 percent interval.
3. Screenshot of the same two parties with only the economic dimension shown, on the same vertical scale.

## Post 3, descriptives carousel (party2d-post3-descriptives.pdf, 6 pages)

1. Title slide reading "What 4,924 party-elections look like".
2. Horizontal bar chart: 59.7 percent of rows combine text and expert evidence, 33.8 percent rest on text only, 4.8 percent on expert only, 1.7 percent are propagated in time.
3. Density plot of 95 percent interval widths across all rows, with the median marked at 0.19 on the cultural dimension and 0.22 on the economic dimension, on a 0 to 1 scale.
4. Horizontal bar chart of the fifteen countries with most election-year rows, led by Denmark with 236, Israel with 218, Belgium with 194 and the Netherlands with 188.
5. Line chart of the German Social Democrats economic position from 1949 to 2025 with a shaded 95 percent interval.
6. Closing slide with the article DOI, the dataset DOI and the explorer link, stating that the figures were recomputed from the released v1 panel.
